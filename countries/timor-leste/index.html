What income is considered high in Timor-Leste?

In this dataset, the 50th-percentile threshold is approximately $1.6K USD per year. The top 10% threshold is approximately $4.6K USD. The top 5% threshold is approximately $6.7K USD. The top 1% threshold is approximately $15.3K USD. These figures describe the statistical income concept used by WID and should not be read as take-home salary or a legal definition of income class.

How the Timor-Leste percentile estimate works

RankMyIncome compares the amount you enter with published WID pre-tax national-income thresholds for equal-split adults. When your income falls between two available threshold points, the calculator estimates the percentile with logarithmic interpolation. Values beyond the published range require extrapolation and are therefore more uncertain.

Local rank vs worldwide rank

The Timor-Leste result uses the local distribution directly. For the worldwide result, the entered USD amount is converted to a common USD-PPP basis using WID's purchasing-power factor for the listed year, then compared with the WID worldwide distribution. This is different from converting money with a market exchange rate.

What do RankMyIncome tiers mean?

The tier names are a visual ranking system created by RankMyIncome, not official WID categories. Diamond begins at the top 10%, Master at the top 5%, Grandmaster at the top 1%, and Challenger at the top 0.1%.

Data notes

The active Timor-Leste income distribution is from 2024, while the purchasing-power conversion uses the 2024 factor. Income distributions and PPP factors can be revised, so the calculator should be treated as an estimate rather than an exact statement about an individual.
